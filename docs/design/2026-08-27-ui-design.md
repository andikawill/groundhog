## Second pass — why the first one read as a debug dump

The first pass was verified as a screenshot at about 60%, and then as measured geometry. Both
tests answer "is anything broken", and everything was in its place. Neither answers "does this
look considered", and the verdict on the built screen — twice — was that it does not. That is
the correct verdict, and here is what was actually wrong with it.

**Nothing had a size.** The largest text on the run screen was 15px, the step id in the pane
head. Everything else lived between 11 and 13. The payload — the thing the whole product
exists to show you — was set at 12px, the same size as the `3 values` count beside it, the
durations in the rail, and the correlation headers. A screen where the evidence and its
footnotes weigh the same is a dump of everything the engine knows, printed in the order it was
collected. The fix is the whole of the type scale below: 11 and 12 are demoted to reference
(labels, durations, path/token pairs), 13 becomes everything read as language or typed into
including the payload, and 16 and 22 are new territory for the two subjects a screen has —
whether the run passed, and which step you are looking at.

**Identifiers stood where names belong.** The header offered `food-journal.case.json` and
`staging.env.json`, and the pane titled itself `save`. Meanwhile `RunStep.title` — `"Simpan
entri jurnal"`, written by whoever wrote the case — was in the response, in state, and thrown
away by the view. The pane now leads with the title and keeps the id in an eyebrow above it,
because the id is a reference (it is what `{{ctx.*}}` and `needs` name) and a reference is not
a heading. The pickers strip the suffix and offer `food-journal` and `staging`. See
[what the pickers still cannot say](#what-the-api-would-have-to-return).

**Six regions, six borders, no elevation.** `.evidence` — 1px, 6px radius — sat inside `.pane`
— 1px, 6px radius — twenty pixels away: two concentric rounded rectangles, which is the look
of a form and not of a record. The rail was four unrelated blocks separated by a 16px gap and
an 11px uppercase word. Now: the pane has no border and no surface of its own, the page's own
background is the background, and the evidence card is the one raised object in the column.
The rail is a divided list — 24px of rhythm and a hairline where the subject changes — behind
a single full-height rule that separates context from record.

**The page stopped where the content stopped.** `align-items: start` plus `align-content:
start` on `.app__body` — the previous fix for phantom gaps — made both columns hug their
content, so on a two-step run the pane's border ended a third of the way down and the rest of
the window was empty. Both problems have one answer: one explicit `1fr` content row. It takes
the leftover height, which is why the rail's rule now reaches the bottom of the window, and it
leaves no auto track to share slack with, which is why the gaps do not come back.

**Micro-typography was unconsidered.** 11px uppercase at weight 500 and 0.06em was the only
structural device on the screen. `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums` was set on `.st__label`,
which contains no digits, and unset on the anchor, the seed, the payload and the matrix. Three
letter-spacing values were hardcoded at three sites. Now: two tracking tokens, one grouped
rule listing every place a digit is compared against another digit, and `.label` at 600 and
0.08em — which is what 11px caps need — used as an index rather than as the thing that makes a
region visible.

**A line was the only way anything was separated.** Six of them, all 1px, all `--line` or
`--line-strong`, none meaning anything different from the others. Three are load-bearing now
and the rest are space: under the app header (chrome / content), down the rail (context /
record), and around the evidence card (this is the exhibit). The matrix keeps its outline for
the same reason the card does, and its head/body boundary is `--line-strong` once instead of
the row hairline repeated.

### Decisions from the first pass this reverses

1. **"No shadow tokens. Elevation is a border plus a surface step."** Wrong, and its own
   argument shows why: *a shadow tuned for light looks like dirt on dark* is an argument for
   defining two, exactly as every colour token here is defined twice. A surface step of
   `#ffffff` on `#f7f7f5` is a 1.04:1 difference — it is not elevation, it is a rumour of it,
   and a border pressed into service as elevation is what made six regions look like six
   boxes. There is now one `--shadow-1`, defined per theme, used by three things that sit
   above the page rather than beside it: the app header, the evidence card, the matrix.
2. **"The scale is small on purpose … the existing components already settled on 13px."**
   That sentence describes the constraint the components arrived at, not a decision, and
   taking it as a decision is how the top of the scale ended up at 15 and 18 — two sizes that
   differ from the base by too little to read as hierarchy. `--fs-4` is 16px and `--fs-5` is
   22px. Five sizes still, three weights still; the gaps between them are the change.
3. **`.evidence` had a `--line-strong` border.** A control's edge has to clear 3:1 because you
   aim at it. A card is not aimed at, and a 3.25:1 outline around a 600px box reads as a
   wireframe. The card takes `--line` and the shadow does the lifting.
4. **`--measure: 96ch`.** A 96-character line is longer than any reading-measure guidance and
   it bound nothing at 1280px anyway. 72ch, and `.empty`, `.hint` and `.evidence__none` now
   respect it as well as `.note`.
5. **The envs screen's `<h1>` moved into `EnvsView`.** It was in `app/envs/page.tsx`, above the
   matrix, and `writing to examples/` was a loose line under it. They are one header row — the
   screen's name and where the screen writes — and only the component knows the root.

## Constraints this was designed inside

No new dependency: no Tailwind, no component library, no icon package, no webfont. One
stylesheet, built on custom properties. System font stack; `ui-monospace` for payloads,
seeds and keys. Desktop-first, unbroken at 1280px, mobile ignored. Light and dark, both
designed. Status never carried by colour alone. Text contrast ≥ 4.5:1 in both themes.
Secrets appear only as `***` or `*** set`.

Two consequences worth naming, because they are choices and not accidents:

- **`color-scheme: light dark` is declared on `:root`.** Native `<select>` popups, scrollbars,
  the text caret and form widget chrome then follow the theme with no styling at all. This is
  the whole reason no select needs to be rebuilt as a custom listbox.
- **`<details>`/`<summary>` is the disclosure everywhere** — the raw log rows, expandable
  request and response bodies. Open state, keyboard behaviour and screen-reader semantics
  come free, and no state has to survive a re-render.

## Where this departs from the spec, and why

Read this before the rest. The spec wins by default; each line below is either a placement
change or something the platform would not do.

1. **The authoring chat bar is a reserved slot, not a designed feature.** The spec puts a
   chat bar at the bottom of the case screen ("Bar chat di bagian bawah dipakai untuk
   menambah atau mengubah step, bukan sebagai pusat layar"). `CLAUDE.md` lists
   natural-language authoring under *deliberately absent*. The layout therefore reserves the
   footer row (`.composer`, full width, below both columns) so adding it later moves nothing;
   the conversation itself is not designed. This is the one place the two documents disagree
   and I have honoured the spec's geometry without inventing the spec's feature.
2. **Seed and anchor live in the rail, not the header.** The spec's header holds case name,
   seed, env dropdown and replay. At 1280px a 24-character ISO anchor plus four controls plus
   the project picker wraps the header. Seed and anchor are copy targets that want a
   monospace block with room around them, so they sit at the top of the left rail with the
   run status and the replay button. Everything the spec asked for is present; two items moved
   down one region.
3. **The guard marker in the env dropdown is only partly honoured.** The spec requires envs
   marked `confirm` or `readonly` to be visible in the env dropdown, not only on the Envs
   screen. A native `<option>` cannot carry a styled badge in any cross-browser way, and
   replacing the select with a custom listbox means JavaScript and an accessibility surface
   nobody asked for. Resolution: the guard goes into the option's **text** (`prod · readonly`)
   and a `.guard` chip is rendered beside the closed select, reflecting the current selection.
   The information is in the dropdown; only its typography is not.
4. **Empty matrix cells get a glyph as well as red.** The spec says red plus a count. Colour
   alone is not allowed here, so an empty cell also gets a dashed 2px border, the placeholder
   text `empty`, and a `!` marker element beside the input. A strengthening, not a change.
5. **Two things in the spec are designed although the element inventory omits them**, because
   the data already exists in `RunStep` and dropping them would be losing information the
   engine paid to collect: `trace` (the correlation headers — `x-request-id` and friends —
   which the spec's `## Log` section says to show per step) and `attempts` (shown only when
   greater than 1, which means a `retryUntil` step retried).
6. **The response body is not in the detail pane.** The spec puts method/URL, body sent and
   origins in the right column, and request/response bodies in the raw log panel. Followed
   exactly: the detail pane never shows the response, the log panel always can.

## Tokens

Every value below is a custom property on `:root`, redefined inside
`@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)`. Ratios in the notes column are measured, not
estimated.

### Colour — surfaces and text

| property | light | dark | note |
|---|---|---|---|
| `--bg` | `#f7f7f5` | `#17181b` | page |
| `--surface` | `#ffffff` | `#1e1f23` | panes, cards, inputs |
| `--surface-2` | `#f1f1ee` | `#24262b` | table head, hover, chips, selected row |
| `--surface-sunken` | `#eceae5` | `#131417` | payload blocks, active button |
| `--line` | `#dcdcd6` | `#34363c` | hairline between rows — decorative only |
| `--line-strong` | `#8f8f88` | `#6b6e78` | control borders; 3.25:1 / 3.24:1 on `--surface` |
| `--fg` | `#1b1b19` | `#e6e7e9` | 16.1:1 / 14.4:1 on `--bg` |
| `--fg-muted` | `#5f5f59` | `#a5a8ae` | 6.0:1 / 7.5:1 — labels, meta, secondary text |
| `--fg-faint` | `#6e6e67` | `#93969d` | 4.8:1 / 6.0:1 — the floor. Nothing dimmer carries text |

The light theme is warm neutral (paper), the dark theme is cool neutral (screen), and dark
surfaces **rise** toward the viewer as elevation increases while light surfaces fall. That
is why the dark values are not the light values inverted: an inverted `#f1f1ee` would put
the table head *below* the page, which reads as a hole.

There is no `--fg-disabled` and no opacity-based dimming. Both produce text whose contrast
depends on what happens to be behind it. Dim text is `--fg-muted` or `--fg-faint`, and both
are measured.

### Colour — status and accent

Each status colour ships with a tint used only as a badge or row background behind that same
colour. Every pairing below is ≥ 5.5:1 in light and ≥ 6.8:1 in dark.

| property | light | dark | used by |
|---|---|---|---|
| `--pass` / `--pass-tint` | `#1c6b3c` / `#e2efe6` | `#67c58c` / `#16291d` | passed |
| `--fail` / `--fail-tint` | `#a62823` / `#fbe4e2` | `#f2908a` / `#301b1a` | failed, empty cell, unresolved origin, errors |
| `--warn` / `--warn-tint` | `#7d4c05` / `#fbedd4` | `#dfa856` / `#2e240f` | awaiting, `confirm` guard, truncated body, attempts |
| `--info` / `--info-tint` | `#1d5aa8` / `#e3ecfa` | `#84b3f2` / `#152438` | running, links, selection |
| `--halt` / `--halt-tint` | `#6337a0` / `#ece5f8` | `#bb9aec` / `#241d33` | interrupted, `readonly` guard, secret flag |
| `--link` | `var(--info)` | `var(--info)` | anchors |
| `--focus` | `#1d5aa8` | `#84b3f2` | focus ring, 6.4:1 / 8.2:1 on `--bg` |
| `--btn-solid-bg` / `--btn-solid-fg` | `#1b1b19` / `#f7f7f5` | `#e6e7e9` / `#17181b` | the one primary button |

There is no brand hue. The primary action is the ink colour on the paper colour — dark
button in light theme, light button in dark theme. A tool with one primary action per screen
does not need a brand colour to find it, and inventing one would be inventing a product
decision.

No colour token changed in the second pass. The hierarchy came out of size, weight and space,
which is where hierarchy is supposed to come from; reaching for a new tint would have been
decoration doing a typographer's job. Two pairings did change, because the pane lost its own
`--surface` and its text now sits on `--bg`, so the measured ratios that matter are:

| pairing | where | light | dark |
|---|---|---|---|
| `--fg` on `--bg` | pane title, step names, payload-adjacent prose | 16.1:1 | 14.4:1 |
| `--fg-muted` on `--bg` | rail labels, pane eyebrow, hints | 6.0:1 | 7.5:1 |
| `--fg-muted` on `--surface` | picker labels in the header, labels inside the card | 6.4:1 | 6.9:1 |
| `--fg-muted` on `--surface-2` | table head, evidence head | 5.7:1 | 6.4:1 |
| `--fg` on `--surface-2` | the request line | 15.2:1 | 12.2:1 |
| `--fg` on `--surface-sunken` | the payload | 14.4:1 | 14.9:1 |
| `--fg-faint` on `--bg` | the floor; nothing dimmer carries text | 4.8:1 | 6.0:1 |
| `--line-strong` on `--bg` | a control edge in the rail or pane, non-text | 3.03:1 | 3.49:1 |

The last row is the one to watch. A `.btn` used to sit on `--surface` where its border
measured 3.25:1; in the rail it now sits on `--bg`, where the same border measures 3.03:1. It
clears the 3:1 floor for a control boundary and there is no room left in it — a lighter
`--line-strong`, or a rail with its own surface, would put it under.

`--halt` doing double duty for *interrupted* and for the `readonly` guard is deliberate:
both mean "this stopped short of running". The secret flag borrows it for the same reason it
borrows nothing else — it is the only other state that means "held back".

### Type

| property | value | used for |
|---|---|---|
| `--font-ui` | `system-ui, -apple-system, 'Segoe UI', Roboto, Helvetica, Arial, sans-serif` | everything not a payload |
| `--font-mono` | `ui-monospace, SFMono-Regular, Menlo, Consolas, 'Liberation Mono', monospace` | payloads, seeds, ids, paths, tokens, env keys, status glyphs |
| `--fs-1` | `11px` | uppercase section labels, chips |
| `--fs-2` | `12px` | meta, durations, log rows, path/token pairs |
| `--fs-3` | `13px` | base: prose, controls, payloads, step names |
| `--fs-4` | `16px` | the lead status badge, the wordmark |
| `--fs-5` | `22px` | the subject of the screen |
| `--lh-tight` / `--lh` / `--lh-mono` | `1.25` / `1.5` / `1.55` | headings / prose / monospace |
| `--tracking-label` | `0.08em` | the only uppercase in the interface |
| `--tracking-tight` | `-0.015em` | 16px and up |
| `--fw` / `--fw-mid` / `--fw-bold` | `400` / `500` / `600` | body / labels and headings / emphasis |

Five sizes, three weights, no italics except the `shared` placeholder in an inherited matrix
cell. **The gaps between the sizes are the hierarchy, and they are what the first pass did not
have.** Read the scale in three bands:

- **11 and 12 are reference.** A label, a duration, a correlation header, a path and the token
  that filled it. Nothing a reader has to comprehend as language sits here — this band is for
  things you locate, compare or ignore.
- **13 is everything read or typed.** Prose, controls, step names, and the payload. The payload
  moved up from 12: it is the one artefact on the screen a reader parses line by line, and it
  had no business at the size of the count beside it.
- **16 and 22 are subjects.** A screen has one or two, no more. On the run screen: whether the
  run passed (`.st--lead`, 16px, in the rail) and which step you are looking at
  (`.pane__title`, 22px). On the envs screen: the word `envs`.

Two tracking values, both optical. 11px caps set at default tracking are mush; 22px at default
tracking sets loose. The mono chips (`.method`, `.guard`, `.cell__flag`) keep their own 0.04em,
which is monospace tracking and a different job.

The status glyphs are set in `--font-mono`, not `--font-ui`. Two reasons: the mono stack has
reliable coverage of the glyph set, and identical advance width means every id in the step
list starts at the same x-position without a table. The glyph box is `1.55em` of whatever
`.st` is set to, which is why `.st__glyph` declares no size of its own — at 12px that is a
19px box inside a 34px row, and at `--fs-4` a 25px target. Both measured.

### Space, shape, layout

| property | value | note |
|---|---|---|
| `--sp-1` … `--sp-8` | `2 4 6 8 12 16 24 32` px | everything; no other spacing values appear |
| `--r-1` / `--r-2` / `--r-3` | `2px` / `4px` / `6px` | chips and glyphs / controls / panes |
| `--bw` / `--bw-thick` | `1px` / `2px` | hairline and control borders / state borders and focus |
| `--rail` | `300px`, `280px` at ≤1100px | left column |
| `--header-h` | `52px` | app header min-height |
| `--measure` | `72ch` | prose max width. Payload blocks are exempt |
| `--shadow-1` | three layers, per theme | the app header, the evidence card, the matrix |

One shadow, defined twice. In light it is the object casting onto the page — 4%, 8% and 10% of
the ink colour at 1px, 6px and 24px. In dark it is the darkness pooling behind a surface that
has risen, which needs to be deeper and softer to register at all: 40%, 45% and 55% of black.
Three things use it and each of them is a thing that sits *above* the page rather than beside
it. Nothing else may take it without a reason of the same kind.

The one media query in the stylesheet narrows the rail. It is not a mobile breakpoint. The
review window is about 936px, and at a 300px rail the run block's key/value grid puts the
anchor — `2026-08-20T00:00:00.000Z`, 24 monospace characters — one pixel from wrapping, and a
wrapped ISO timestamp reads as two values. At 280px it measures a single 18px line with room
to spare, and the pane still holds 73 monospace characters at `--fs-3`, which is enough for
pretty-printed JSON. Measured at both widths; at 1280px the rail is 300px and the pane holds
about 120.

The one animation is `gh-pulse` on the running glyph, 1.4s opacity, disabled under
`prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`. Motion is never the only carrier of anything.

## Status semantics

Six statuses. Five come from `RunStatus` in `lib/store/runs.ts`
(`running | awaiting | interrupted | passed | failed`) and three from `StepStatus` in
`lib/engine/types.ts` (`passed | failed | skipped`); the union is six, and the same badge
renders both. That is the point of having one badge: a status in the step list, in the run
status line and in the earlier-runs list is the same word and the same shape.

| status | glyph | colour | shape | reads as |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| passed | `✓` | `--pass` | square, 1px border, tinted fill | done, fine |
| failed | `×` | `--fail` | square, **2px** border, tinted fill | done, not fine |
| skipped | `–` | `--fg-faint` | square, **dashed** border, no fill | never ran, on purpose |
| awaiting | `‖` | `--warn` | **circle**, 1px border, tinted fill | stopped, waiting for you |
| running | `>` | `--info` | **circle**, 1px border, tinted fill, pulsing | still moving |
| interrupted | `!` | `--halt` | square, **dotted** 2px border | stopped, not by you |

The shape language is the thing that survives greyscale: **round means the run is still
alive** (awaiting, running), **square means it is over** (passed, failed, interrupted), and
**broken borders mean nothing was sent** (dashed = skipped by design, dotted = interrupted by
accident). Colour is a third channel on top of glyph and shape, never the first.

Glyphs are single characters from a set the monospace stack covers everywhere: `✓` U+2713,
`×` U+00D7, `–` U+2013, `‖` U+2016, `>` U+003E, `!` U+0021. If `‖` ever proves unreliable,
`||` (two ASCII pipes) is the substitute; nothing else in the set has an alternative and
nothing else needs one.

Markup contract:

```html
<span class="st st--failed">
  <span class="st__glyph" aria-hidden="true">×</span>
  <span class="st__label">failed</span>
</span>
```

`.st__label` is the status word. **It is always in the DOM.** Where the layout has no room
for it — the step list, the earlier-runs list — it is `.visually-hidden`, not omitted. A
glyph alone is a shape to a sighted reader and nothing at all to a screen reader.

One modifier: `.st--lead`, added in the second pass, sets the badge to `--fs-4` and its label to
`--fw-mid`. It marks a status that is the *subject* of a region rather than a cell in a list —
the run's own status in the rail, the shown step's status in the pane head. Two per screen at
most, and never in a list. `<Badge lead />` in `RunView.tsx` is the only way it is applied.

### How a step row composes

```html
<li>
  <button class="step" aria-current="true">
    <span class="st st--failed">…</span>      <!-- col 1: max-content -->
    <span class="step__id">save</span>         <!-- col 2: minmax(0,1fr), mono -->
    <span class="step__meta">                  <!-- col 3: max-content, tabular -->
      <span class="step__attempts">3×</span> 1.2s
    </span>
  </button>
</li>
```

Three columns, baseline-aligned. The whole row is the button, so the click target is the row
and the focus ring outlines the row. Selection is `aria-current="true"`, which drives both
the background and the bold id — one attribute, no `selected` class to keep in sync with it.

`.step__attempts` renders only when `RunStep.attempts > 1`, in `--warn`, because a step that
needed four tries to pass is not the same news as one that passed.

Two statuses are **derived, not stored.** `StepStatus` has no `awaiting` or `running` member
and should not gain one. The run screen already knows the run status and the length of
`results`, so:

- run status `awaiting` → the step at `results.length` renders as an `awaiting` row
- run status `running` → the step at `results.length` renders as a `running` row
- run status `interrupted` → no derived row; interrupted is shown on the run status line and
  in the earlier-runs list. A step that never started has no row

The derived row needs the step id, which means reading `caseSnapshot.steps` — which the run
screen should be reading anyway, since the spec is explicit that the log view reads the
snapshot rather than the live case. Until it does, the derived row can render with the
status and no id, and that is a visible gap rather than a wrong id.

## Screens

Four layouts. Three of them are the same shell.

The shell: `.app` (header row + body row) → `.app__header` (flex, sticky) → `.app__body`
(`grid-template-columns: var(--rail) minmax(0,1fr)`, or `.app__body--wide` for one column).

`.app__body` declares `grid-template-rows: 1fr` and carries no padding of its own. Both of
those are load-bearing:

- **One explicit row** takes the leftover height of the page, so the rail's dividing rule runs
  to the bottom of the window on a run with two steps in it. It also leaves no auto track to
  share slack with, which is the earlier phantom-gap fix kept rather than traded away. A
  composer row added later is implicit, therefore auto, and sits under this one.
- **No padding** means each column carries its own, so the rule between them meets the header
  at the top and the window at the bottom instead of floating in a 24px inset. Measured at
  936×900: rail 280 and pane 656, both 848px tall, no sideways overflow. At 1280: 300 and 980.

The header is `position: sticky`. In a grid whose row is auto-sized this is the pattern that
often silently does nothing, so it was checked rather than assumed: at `scrollY: 400` the
header's `top` is `0`.

Header, left to right: wordmark, project picker, case picker, env picker with its guard chip,
the primary `run` button, then nav to `envs` and `cases` pushed right by `.spacer`. Project
first, env after — changing project changes the case list and the Envs screen, so it reads
outside-in.

Each picker carries a visible `.picker__label` — `case`, `env` — rather than an `aria-label`.
Two adjacent unlabelled selects are a guess for everyone, and the words cost 40px. The
`<label>` wrapper is the association, so neither element needs an id.

**The pickers offer names, not paths.** `nameOf()` strips `.case.json` / `.env.json`, so the
options read `food-journal` and `staging`; the option's `value` is still the path, so nothing
about what gets POSTed changes. This is a presentation fix for a data problem — see
[what the API would have to return](#what-the-api-would-have-to-return).

Rail, top to bottom: the run block (status badge, seed, anchor, env, replay, copy markdown),
then the error slot, then the pause actions when they apply, then steps, then earlier runs.
Fixed order. A control never moves between states; it appears or it is absent.

The regions are divided, not boxed. Every direct child of `.rail` is a `.stack` — including the
error, which is wrapped for exactly this reason — and the rhythm is stated once, on the
container: 24px of `padding-block`, zero on the first and last, and a `--line` hairline on
`.stack + .stack`. Four bordered cards in a column is an inventory; this is a list. Behind all
of it, `.rail` has one `border-right` running the full height of the window: context on the
left, the record on the right.

Pane head, three lines deep and in this order:

1. `.pane__eyebrow` — `step 4 of 6`, then `·` and the step's **id** in mono. Position first,
   because a name with no position tells you nothing about where you are in a run. Both are
   reference, so both sit at `--fs-2`. The id appears here only when there is a title to put
   above it; with no title it would be printing the heading twice.
2. `.pane__title` — the step's **declared title** at `--fs-5`, falling back to the id.
   `RunStep.title` is already stored by the engine and already returned by `/api/runs/[id]`;
   the first pass simply did not read it.
3. The status badge, `.st--lead`, sharing the title's baseline. It is a *column* of
   `.pane__heading`'s grid rather than a sibling of the block, because a flex row aligned on
   baselines takes the block's **first** baseline — which is the eyebrow's, a line above where
   the badge belongs.

**The evidence region is the centre of this design.** One bordered box, `--line`, lifted by
`--shadow-1`, no internal gaps: the request line, the body actually sent, and the origins list
are three sections of a single exhibit, sharing borders. The reason is the brief's reason —
this is the line that separates "the product sent the wrong payload" from "I mistyped a token"
— and a region that has to be believed cannot be laid out like a footnote under a body block.
It is the only raised object in the column, which is what makes it the exhibit rather than one
card among several.

Four things make it read as evidence:

1. **The origins list is inside the same box as the body**, directly beneath it, sharing its
   border. Nothing sits between them. You look at a value and its origin without moving your
   eyes to another card. `.code--flush` draws only a `border-bottom` for this: the head's own
   `border-bottom` is already the line above the body, and drawing both doubled a 1px rule
   into 2px.
2. **Three grid columns, so every `←` lines up down the pane.** `minmax(8ch, max-content)` for
   the path, `2ch` for the arrow, `1fr` for the token. Alignment is what makes twelve rows
   scannable instead of read one at a time. Zebra striping on odd rows keeps long paths
   attached to their tokens.
3. **The token carries the weight, not the path.** Path in `--fg-muted`, token in `--fg` at
   `--fw-mid`. The reader's question is "what filled this?", so the answer gets the emphasis.
4. **Absence is stated, not implied.** A step with no tokens renders the region with
   `.evidence__none`: *"no tokens — every value here was written literally."* An empty list
   that disappears looks like a missing feature; this is a real answer, and it is the answer
   `literal by default` promises.

The unresolved-reference row, `.origin--missing`, is the payoff: `--fail-tint` background, a
2px `--fail` inset left bar, the arrow glyph swapped from `←` to `✗`, and the missing name
after the token. This is the row that says *the step was skipped and here is the exact
reference that skipped it* — the invariant that a skipped step names its reference, made
visible. Colour, glyph and position all change, so it survives greyscale.

Failures use the same inset-bar treatment in the assertions list (`.assert--failed`). Two
places, one visual idea: a 2px bar on the left edge of the row means *this is the thing that
went wrong*.

The assertion row is `--fs-3` for the expression and `--fs-2` for `.assert__detail`, and the
detail declares `grid-column: 2`. It has to: as the third child of a two-column grid it landed
in column 1, under the badge, where it widened a `max-content` track and knocked every glyph
in the list out of line. Measured after: the detail's left edge and the expression's left edge
are the same pixel.

`.truncated` sits inside the evidence box above the body when `RawResponse.truncated` is set,
in `--warn-tint` with a `!` — a body cut at `MAX_BODY_BYTES` must never look like a complete
one.

## States each screen must handle

Nothing below gets a spinner. A spinner tells you the app is alive; these tell you what is
happening. `.empty`, `.note` and `.error` cover all of them.

### Run view

The rail is a fixed column in every one of these states, including the ones where it has
nothing in it. An empty 280px column with a rule down its edge is how a two-pane tool looks
before you have opened anything; a rail that appeared with the first run would move the whole
record sideways at the exact moment a reader started looking at it.

| state | rail | pane |
|---|---|---|
| first load, nothing run | pickers populated, `run` enabled, no run block, no steps | `.empty`: "pick a case and press run. nothing has been sent yet." |
| loading the file lists | pickers disabled, `run` disabled | same empty text |
| run starting | run block with `⟨> running⟩`, seed and anchor as soon as the POST returns | `.empty`: "waiting for the first step" |
| running, steps arriving | rows append; the derived `running` row sits at the end | last completed step, auto-selected |
| awaiting at a pause | pause actions + the credential hint | last completed step; `edit body` enabled |
| passed | `⟨✓ passed⟩`, replay + copy markdown | selected step, or the last one |
| failed | `⟨× failed⟩`, replay + copy markdown | **first failed step auto-selected**, not the last step. The first failure is the cause; the ones after it are consequences |
| interrupted | `⟨! interrupted⟩`, replay; a `.note` saying it will not resume by itself | last recorded step |
| pre-flight refusal (empty env var, `readonly` guard, `confirm` guard) | `.error` with the server's message, no run block, `run` still enabled | unchanged empty state |
| resume race (`already resumed elsewhere`) | `.error`, pause actions removed | unchanged |
| no earlier runs | the `EARLIER RUNS` label and list are both absent | — |
| run opened from history | identical to its terminal state, plus a `.note`: "opened from history. replay reads the case and env files as they are now." | selected step |

The last row matters more than it looks. A run opened from history is not distinguishable from
a run that just finished unless the screen says so, and the difference is exactly the one the
existing `page.tsx` copy already warns about: replay re-reads the files, resume does not.

## Rules a later screen must follow

Thirteen rules. They exist so screen five does not need a retrofit. The first four are the
second pass's; a screen can satisfy every one of the others and still read as a debug dump if
it breaks these.

**Hierarchy.** Every screen has one subject and states it at `--fs-5`. Whatever answers the
question the screen exists to answer takes `--fs-4`. Everything read as language or typed into
is `--fs-3`. 11 and 12 are for things you locate, compare or ignore. If two things at different
levels of the page are the same size, one of them is wrong — and if the screen's most important
artefact is at `--fs-2`, the screen is a log.

**Space separates, a line means something.** Reach for `--sp-7` between regions before reaching
for a border. A hairline is allowed where the subject changes — between siblings in a list, at
a head/body boundary, around a raised card — and nowhere else. **Never nest a border inside a
border.** If a region needs an outline to be found, the layout around it has failed and an
outline will not fix it.

**Elevation.** `--shadow-1`, and only for something that sits above the page: chrome, or the one
object on the screen that has to be believed. A surface step alone is not elevation — `#ffffff`
on `#f7f7f5` is 1.04:1. Two raised objects on one screen means neither is raised.

**Names, not identifiers.** A product surface shows what a thing is called. An identifier is
reference: it belongs in an eyebrow, a mono column or a `title`, never as a heading, and never
as the only thing on offer in a picker. Before rendering a path or a filename, check whether the
data already carries a name — twice now, it did.

**Tables.** `.table` inside `.table-scroll`. Header cells uppercase `--fs-1` `--fg-muted` on
`--surface-2`, sticky top, with a `--line-strong` bottom border — the end of the header is not a
row separator. Row separators `--line`, never a vertical grid. First column sticky when the
table scrolls sideways, and it carries the row's name at `--fs-3`. Numeric columns
right-aligned; `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums` is already set on `.table`. Totals and
counts in `tfoot`, never floating above the table.

**Inputs.** `.input`, or `.input--mono` when the content is a payload, a template, a path or
a key. Border `--line-strong` — not `--line`; a control's edge has to clear 3:1. Invalid is
`aria-invalid="true"`, which paints the border, never a colour class. Every input has a
label: `.field__label`, or `.visually-hidden` when the column head is the label.

**Buttons.** `.btn` is the default. `.btn--primary` is the solid one and there is **at most
one per screen region** — `run` on the run screen, `continue` at a pause. `.btn--ghost` for
inline text actions. `.btn--xs` for anything inside a pane head or a table row. Disabled is
the `disabled` attribute, never a class and never `pointer-events: none`, which also removes
the tooltip and the focus.

**Monospace blocks.** `.code`: `--surface-sunken`, `--fs-3`, `white-space: pre-wrap`,
`overflow-wrap: anywhere`, `max-height: 26em`, then scroll. A payload never stretches a pane
to the height of the document, and it never truncates without saying so. `.code--flush` when
it sits inside a bordered region: the region's edges are the box, so the block draws only the
line dividing it from what follows.

**Numbers.** Any digit a reader compares against another digit is tabular. The rule listing
every such place is at the top of the stylesheet — add to that list rather than setting
`font-variant-numeric` at a new site. Prose keeps proportional figures.

**Hit targets.** 24px minimum, and `min-height` says so rather than being arrived at by adding
up padding and line-height. `.btn--xs`, `.cell__flag` and `.tag` all sit exactly on the floor;
anything smaller than `--fs-2` with `--sp-1` padding will fall through it silently.

**Copy affordances.** A `.btn--xs` with a text label — `copy curl`, `copy markdown`. No icon,
because there is no icon package and a glyph nobody recognises is worse than four words. On
success the label becomes `copied` and `data-copied="true"` for about 1.2s, which paints it
`--pass`. Never a toast. Copy buttons live at the level they copy: run-level in a pane head,
step-level in the step's own row or pane head.

**Status.** Always the `.st` badge. Never a bare coloured dot, never colour on the text alone.
The label is in the DOM even when it is hidden.

**Secrets.** A secret is `***` or `*** set`, and nothing anywhere reveals one. When designing
a new screen: if a control would need the plaintext to work, the control is wrong, not the
rule.

**Focus.** `:focus-visible` gives every interactive element a 2px `--focus` outline at 2px
offset, globally. Do not remove it, do not replace it with a border change, do not scope it
away. When a whole row is clickable, the row is the button, so the ring outlines the row.

**Colour is never alone.** Every state that means something carries a glyph, a shape, a border
style or a word in addition to its colour. The test: screenshot it, desaturate it, and see
whether it still reads.

## What the API would have to return

Three things on these screens are identifiers standing in for names, and none of them can be
fixed in the stylesheet or the view. Each is a change to what a route returns, and each is
small.

1. **`/api/files` lists filenames.** The pickers strip `.case.json` / `.env.json` and offer
   `food-journal` and `staging`, which is honest but is not the name the case declares —
   `TestCase.name` and `EnvDef.name` are inside the files. If the route returned
   `{ file, name }` per entry, the option text becomes the declared name and the `value` stays
   the path, so nothing else changes. The cost is opening each file to read one field.
2. **The env picker has no guard.** [Departure 3](#where-this-departs-from-the-spec-and-why)
   requires `staging · confirm` in the option text and a `.guard` chip beside the closed
   select. The guard is in the env file and `/api/files` does not read it; `/api/envs` has it
   but the run screen does not call that route. Same fix as above with a third field:
   `{ file, name, guard }`.
3. **The run screen never names the case being looked at.** `GET /api/runs/[id]` already
   returns `caseName` — the declared name, not the path — and the view drops it: `open()` keeps
   `seed`, `anchorAt` and `envName` in `meta` and nothing else. One more field on that object
   and the rail's run block can lead with the case's name, which is the honest `<h1>` for the
   screen. This is a state change rather than a new fetch, so this pass left it alone.

Until (3) lands, the run screen's `<h1>` is the step's title in the pane head. That is a
defensible subject for the column — it is what the pane is about — but the screen as a whole is
about a run of a case, and the case's name is not on it anywhere except inside a picker.

## What I deliberately did not design

- **Mobile and tablet.** Out of scope by the brief. The layout is a two-column grid with one
  breakpoint's worth of tokens; a later pass collapses `.app__body` to one column and un-sticks
  the matrix. Nothing here blocks that. The `max-width: 1100px` query that narrows the rail is
  not that pass and is not a mobile breakpoint — it is one token holding its narrowest legal
  content, and it is the only media query in the stylesheet besides
  `prefers-color-scheme` and `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- **A manual light/dark switch.** `prefers-color-scheme` was what was asked for, and a switch
  means a persisted preference, a `data-theme` attribute and a flash-of-wrong-theme problem on
  first paint. When it is wanted, the token blocks move behind `[data-theme]` and no other rule
  changes.
- **The authoring conversation.** The footer slot is reserved and named; message bubbles, a
  streaming indicator and a step-proposal form are not designed, because the feature is listed
  as deliberately absent in `CLAUDE.md`. See departure 1.
- **Step reordering in editor mode.** Drag-and-drop is a dependency or a day of work, and it is
  not in the inventory. The editor list is selectable, not sortable.
- **A distinct treatment for `semantic` assertions.** `AssertResult` is
  `{ expr, ok, detail }` with no field saying who judged it, so a badge marking LLM verdicts
  would be inventing data. The assertion row is the same either way. If the field arrives, it
  is a `.tag--static` chip in the row, no new layout.
- **Empty, loading and error illustrations.** A sentence that names the missing thing and the
  directory it should be in is more use than a graphic, and it costs no bytes.
- **Toasts, modals, tooltips and dropdown menus.** Not one of the screens needs a layer above
  the page. `edit body` edits in place, errors appear where the failure was, and every menu on
  these screens is a native `<select>` that themes itself. The first feature that genuinely
  needs a layer should design one; three screens do not.
- **Print styles, `@container` queries, view transitions.** No reader asked for any of them.
